Your worksheet:

How to make your worksheet

  1. Pick the reading level, and type a topic such as dog, rain or cake. The stories that fit are listed.
  2. Or choose “Type my own story” and type it, one line on each row.
  3. Choose the text size, picture words, questions and a drawing box.
  4. Pick a design and print. To keep a copy, choose “Save as PDF” as the printer.

Stories that match what was taught

Each story in the library was written for one step of learning to read, from short words such as cat and mud up to vowel teams such as oa and ow. Every word in it was checked against the letter sounds taught by that step, plus a few common words learned by sight.

Two voices

In your own story, start a row with G: and the grown-up reads that line. It is printed small in a soft box. The other lines are for the child and are printed big. Taking turns lets a new reader enjoy a richer story and still read every word of the big lines.

Picture words

A picture word is shown as a small crayon picture with the word under it. In your own story, put the word in brackets, like [cat]. It works for every word in our picture library; other words stay as words.

Nothing is written by a machine

The maker never invents a story. When no story fits your topic, it says so and shows the nearest ones.

Good questions

Is this an AI story generator?

No. It searches a library of stories that people wrote and checked word by word, and it prints what you type yourself. Nothing is made up on the spot, so no odd or wrong sentence can appear.

What if there is no story about my topic?

The page tells you so and lists the nearest stories. You can also choose “Type my own story”.

Can I type my own story?

Yes. Type one line on each row. Start a row with G: for a line the grown-up reads, and put a word in brackets to show it as a picture. What you type stays on your device.

Does it check the reading level of my story?

When you pick a reading level, the page lists the words in the child’s lines that have not been taught by that level. It is only a note. The story prints as you typed it.

Are there questions?

Yes. Each library story has three simple questions. Print them with lines to write on, or as questions to talk about.
