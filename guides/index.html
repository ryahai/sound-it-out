The sign is that they can hold crayons with their fingers instead of a fist. Teach the tripod grip: thumb and index finger near the tip, pencil resting on the middle finger. What Is a Sight Word?

What the parent saysThe teacher keeps saying sight words. What is a sight word?What we foundA sight word is a word a child reads right away, without stopping to sound it out, such as the, and, is, said, was and you. Most words get there by being sounded out many times. A few very common words, like “the”, “said” and “was”, have one part that does not follow the usual sounds. Children do not need to memorize these as whole shapes. They match each sound to its letter or letters and learn only the tricky part by heart.Open the case file →

When Should a Child Know All Their Letters?

What the parent saysShould my child know all the letters by now?What we foundMany children know all 26 uppercase letters by the end of kindergarten, around age 5 or 6. Many know all 26 lowercase letters by the end of kindergarten or early first grade. Lowercase letters take longer because some, like b, d, p and q, look alike. A child does not need all 26 before starting to read. With the first few letter sounds, a child can already blend short words. Children differ in pace, so slower progress at age 5 or 6 is not, on its own, a sign of a problem.Open the case file →
