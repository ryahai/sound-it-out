The goal

On day 10 your child reads a short sentence they have never seen, smoothly, the way they would say it.

That is the goal, not a promise. Some children are smooth by day 7, and some need the ten days twice. Reading word by word is how almost every new reader starts.

First, find where the reading sticks

A child can read slowly for four different reasons, and each needs a different push. Day 1 is a three-minute check, with no timer, that points to the most likely one.

What this is not

It is not a promise. Some children are smooth by day 7. Some need the ten days twice. It is not speed reading: there is no timer, and nobody counts words per minute. It is not a test for dyslexia or anything else; if your child is seven or older and reading is still very slow and tiring, tell the teacher too. It is not an app, and your child does not need a screen. It uses short words that can be sounded out, so a child who cannot yet blend sounds into words should start with the blending plan. You need a printer.

Questions

Do I need a printer?

Yes. The pages are made to be printed once, on plain paper. The black-and-white copy uses very little ink.

Is it normal to read one word at a time?

Yes. Almost every new reader does. Sounding out takes all of their attention, so there is none left for making it flow. It usually gets better as short words become easy. This plan is the practice that helps that happen. If your child is seven or older and reading is still very slow and tiring, do the plan and tell the teacher what you see.

Will this make my child read faster?

Smoother, which is the part that matters. We do not time children and we do not set a speed, because parents told us pressure makes it worse. When short words come all at once and words are read in groups, reading takes less effort. That is the aim. We never measure speed.

My child can read words but not sentences. Is this for us?

Yes, that is one of the four sticking points the day 1 check looks for. The plan goes from single words to scoops of two or three words, then to a sentence built one word at a time, then to whole sentences. Not sure? Do the free check.

How old should my child be?

Most children who need this are five to seven. Your child should already be able to sound out short words such as cat, hen and mud. If that is still hard, start with the blending plan.

How much of my time does it take?

About two minutes at the start of each day’s page, and a minute at the end when your child reads to you. Day 1 and day 10 are checks of about three minutes each.

What if my child cannot do a day?

Stop, and do the same page again tomorrow. Reading the same page twice is the method here, not a step back. The halfway check on day 5 tells you whether to go on or go back.

Does anything record my child?

No. Nothing in this pack watches, listens to or records your child. You are the one who listens.

How do I get the videos and sound tracks?

The “start here” page of the book has a code to scan with your phone, and a short link. They open in your browser. There is nothing to install.
