The most common mistake: Telling the answer at every slip
Not like thisYour child reads dad as bad. You stop them and say: that is a d.
Like thisYou wait for the end of the line. Your child checks with the bed.

What to say: “Check your b and d with the bed.”

Day 1

Find the Mix-Up

About three minutes. Listen and mark. 0:51 min

  • A check, not a lesson: listen and mark.
  • Letters to read, letters to write, then six words.
  • Keep the page for day 10.

If your child gets stuck today, say: “Say the ones you know. It is fine to stop and think.”

What today’s check tells you: Your child fits one line in the box on your page. Stop at the first one. A 11 or 12 right and B 5 or 6 right means start at day 8.

If the check was hard to do

If both A and B are low, do every day in order, and stay on day 2 until b is steady before you start day 3. If your child got tired, stop and do part C tomorrow. Do not teach or correct on the check.

Day 2

Only b: Bat First, Then Ball

One letter today. Only one. 0:49 min

  • Only one letter today.
  • Bat first (the line), then the ball.
  • Your child says it out loud each time.

If your child gets stuck today, say: “Bat first, then ball. Start with the line going down. Now go around.”

Ready for day 3 when: Your child wrote b three times for you at the end, each with the line first, saying bat, then ball.

Day 4

The Bed

The two letters meet. 0:45 min

  • The two letters meet today.
  • The word bed looks like a bed: head and foot.
  • Not sure? Write bed and look.

If your child gets stuck today, say: “Write bed in the corner and look. The b is the head of the bed. The d is the foot.”

Ready for day 5 when: Your child wrote b, d and bed for you at the end, with both letters facing the right way.
